What subjexts shows and stores

The student notice (docs: "Day 2, the student notice").

What you see

On your course book's pages, subjexts shows what your instructor marked for your section: passages covered in class, passages to read on your own, passages skipped, and the instructor's comments. The same list, by class meeting or by chapter, is on the What we covered page.

You don't need to sign in for any of that. The section is set by the link your instructor posted and remembered in your browser; you can change or turn it off in the panel (the subjexts button at the bottom right of a page).

What is stored about you

  • Nothing, until you sign in. Reading a book with subjexts sends no information about you anywhere. The panel loads the section's coverage marks and keeps a copy in your browser so they show even when the service is down.
  • When you sign in (with GitHub, later also Google or Microsoft), the service keeps the name and email your sign-in provider gives it, and your section membership.
  • What you write is stored: private notes and highlights (visible only to you), messages to your instructor (visible to you and your section's staff), suggested edits (visible to the book's authors and your section's staff), and later answers to practice questions.
  • No tracking, no advertising, no third-party analytics. Other users see only display names, never emails.

Who can see what

  • Instructors of your section see your messages and, if the section's settings say so, your questions to the AI tutor (when that exists). They never see your private notes.
  • Authors of a book see suggested edits (with your name only if you opt in) and anonymous totals per passage. They never see enrollments, names, messages or grades.
  • The platform's administrator can see everything except private notes.

Where

Data is stored with Supabase in a United States region. Books stay on their authors' websites; subjexts adds a layer on top and never copies them.
